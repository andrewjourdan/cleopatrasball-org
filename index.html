The Four Hebs

William Shakespeare's tragedy of Rome and Egypt, staged at every change of season along the road Egypt's obelisks travelled. The play opens in Manhattan in spring, crosses to London for summer and Paris for autumn, then comes home to Luxor and the pyramids of Giza in winter.

Follow the migration See the beneficiaries

Heb (ḥb) is the ancient Egyptian word for festival, as in the Heb Sed, the jubilee a pharaoh celebrated to renew his reign. Each season of this tour is one heb.

The route

Following the obelisks home

Three obelisks left Egypt in the 1800s. Two came from Heliopolis and now stand in Central Park and on the Thames, both called Cleopatra's Needle. The third left the gate of Luxor Temple for the Place de la Concorde, and its twin still stands in Luxor today. The Four Hebs travel that road in reverse, one city per season, and finish where the stones began.

Heb I · Spring equinox · 20 March 2027

ManhattanCentral Park · The Metropolitan Museum of Art

The scarab. Khepri, the scarab god, rolled the sun into the sky each morning. Spring opens the migration under his sign.

More than twenty spring evenings at the Delacorte Theater, each one followed by a short walk across the park to the obelisk that has stood behind the Met since 1881.

  1. 8:00 pm

    The play

    Antony and Cleopatra on the Delacorte's open-air stage, 1,800 seats facing Turtle Pond and Belvedere Castle. Proposed with The Public Theater, which operates the Delacorte and has staged Shakespeare there since 1962.

  2. 10:45 pm

    The light show

    A short illumination of Cleopatra's Needle, the granite obelisk of Thutmose III on Greywacke Knoll, a few minutes' walk east of the theater.

  3. 11:15 pm

    The ball

    Drinks and dancing in the Sackler Wing around the Temple of Dendur, a sandstone temple built about 15 BC and given to the United States by Egypt in 1965.

Sample evening. Times are illustrative.

Heb II · Summer solstice · 21 June 2027

LondonVictoria Embankment · The British Museum

The falcon. Horus, the falcon of the sky, watches over the season when the sun stands highest.

The play crosses the Atlantic to the Thames and is staged beside London's Cleopatra's Needle, the sister stone of the Central Park obelisk, with the roses of the Victoria Embankment Gardens in summer bloom behind the audience.

  1. Sunset

    The play by the river

    Antony and Cleopatra performed on the Embankment between the needle's two bronze sphinxes, with the river and the South Bank as the backdrop.

  2. Daytime

    Companion visits

    Ticket holders are pointed to the British Museum's Egyptian sculpture galleries, a short walk north of the Embankment.

  3. 2 August

    The eclipse from the Embankment

    About 42% of the sun is covered as seen from London. A public viewing at Cleopatra's Needle, with eclipse glasses handed out, while patrons watch totality in Luxor.

  4. 31 August

    The Black Moon

    A performance at dusk on the night of August's second new moon, with the Needle lit against the darkest sky of the month.

Interlude · Total solar eclipse · 2 August 2027

The Eclipse Expedition

In the middle of the London summer, patrons fly to Luxor for the longest total solar eclipse over land this century, sail north on the Nile to Cairo and Giza, and return to London for the Black Moon performance on 31 August.

Heb III · Autumn equinox · 23 September 2027

ParisPlace de la Concorde · Bois de Boulogne · The Louvre

The Egyptian Mau. The spotted cat of the tomb paintings, sacred to Bastet. Paris takes the cat.

Autumn begins at the Obélisque de Louxor, the one stone on the route that came from Luxor itself. The play moves to a nineteenth-century garden theater in the Bois de Boulogne, and the season closes under the glass pyramid of the Louvre.

  1. Opening

    The obelisk lit

    The season opens at the Obélisque de Louxor on the Place de la Concorde, raised there in 1836 after leaving the gate of Luxor Temple.

  2. Evenings

    The play in the garden

    Antony and Cleopatra at the Théâtre de Verdure du Jardin Shakespeare in the Pré Catelan, an open-air stage of about 250 seats set among gardens planted after five Shakespeare plays.

  3. Closing

    The ball

    A closing ball beneath the glass pyramid of the Louvre, steps from the museum's Egyptian Antiquities galleries.

Heb IV · Winter solstice · 22 December 2027

ThebesLuxor Museum · Luxor Temple

The jackal. Anubis, guide through the night, leads the winter tour through the temple after dark.

Winter brings the migration to ancient Thebes, where the Paris obelisk's twin still stands at the gate of Luxor Temple. Here the season is a homecoming.

  1. Homecoming

    Luxor-related artifacts return

    An exhibition at the Luxor Museum built around objects with ties to Thebes, brought back as loans or returns arranged with Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities and the lending institutions.

  2. After dark

    The lighted temple

    A lit tour and entry through Luxor Temple, from the pylon of Ramesses II and its standing obelisk to the colonnade of Amenhotep III.

  3. Departure

    The company embarks

    The cast, crew and guests board a Nile expedition boat at the Luxor corniche to begin the voyage north.

Finale · North on the Nile · Winter 2027–28

GizaThe Nile expedition · The pyramid complex

The Nile perch. The great fish of the river. The Upper Egyptian town of Esna was called Latopolis, city of the perch.

From Luxor the company sails north, downstream, on a Nile expedition boat. The voyage ends at the Giza Plateau, where Antony and Cleopatra is staged for the last time and all three pyramids are lit.

  1. The voyage

    Luxor to Giza by river

    The Nile flows north, so the migration rides the current from Thebes toward the Delta, with stops planned along the way.

  2. Final nights

    The play at the pyramids

    The closing performances on a stage within the Giza pyramid complex, with the Great Sphinx below the plateau.

  3. Last curtain

    Three pyramids lit

    Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure lit together, the last light show of the migration.
