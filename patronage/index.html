The journey · Proposed itinerary

London to Luxor, north on the Nile, and home for the Black Moon

Five chapters across one month of summer. Patrons leave London on 30 July, stand in Luxor for totality on 2 August, sail north with the current to Cairo, and return to London for the Black Moon performance on the night of the month's second new moon.

Chapter I

London to Luxor

  1. Fri 30 JulDepart

    London to Luxor

    Evening flight from London to Luxor and transfer to the expedition's base on the East Bank.

  2. Sat 31 JulLuxor

    Karnak at first light

    The Temple of Amun at Karnak before the heat of the day. In the evening, a private visit to the Luxor Museum.

  3. Sun 1 AugWest Bank

    The Valley of the Kings

    The royal tombs and the Temple of Hatshepsut at dawn. Eclipse-eve dinner and a safe-viewing briefing from the expedition's astronomer.

Chapter II

The eclipse

  1. Mon 2 AugLuxor

    Totality over Thebes

    Luxor sits near the point of greatest eclipse, the place on Earth where totality lasts longest.

    Morning
    A staged reading of Act 3 of Antony and Cleopatra, the scene of the "terrene moon."
    Late morning
    The partial phase begins. Certified eclipse glasses on.
    1:02 pm
    Mid-totality. About six minutes and nineteen seconds of darkness; glasses off only on the guide's call.
    Afternoon
    The sun returns. Glasses back on until the last of the moon has passed.
    Evening
    A lighted night at Luxor Temple, then patrons board the expedition vessel.
Chapter III

North with the current

  1. Tue 3 AugDendera

    The Temple of Hathor

    Cleopatra VII and her son Caesarion are carved on the temple's rear wall. Inside is the zodiac ceiling, whose original relief has been in the Louvre since the 1820s, where the tour arrives in autumn.

  2. Wed 4 AugAbydos

    The Temple of Seti I

    The King List of Abydos and the Osireion, the sanctuary of Osiris.

  3. Thu 5 AugSohag

    The Red Monastery

    A painted early Christian church, conserved by the American Research Center in Egypt.

  4. Fri 6 AugAsyut

    A day on the river

    Sailing through Middle Egypt. Readings and rehearsals on deck at sunset.

  5. Sat 7 AugAmarna

    Tell el-Amarna

    The capital Akhenaten built for the sun disk, the Aten.

  6. Sun 8 AugMinya

    Beni Hasan and Tuna el-Gebel

    The painted rock-cut tombs of Beni Hasan and the catacombs of Thoth at Tuna el-Gebel.

  7. Mon 9 AugSailing

    Toward the north

    A second day on the river as the valley widens toward Cairo.

  8. Tue 10 AugBeni Suef

    The pyramid at Meidum

    The pyramid of Sneferu, the first attempt at a true pyramid.

Chapter IV

Cairo and Giza

  1. Wed 11 AugCairo

    Arrival and Saqqara

    Patrons leave the vessel in Cairo. Afternoon at Saqqara and the Step Pyramid of Djoser.

  2. Thu 12 AugGiza

    The pyramids and the Grand Egyptian Museum

    The Giza Plateau in the morning, the Grand Egyptian Museum in the afternoon, and a farewell dinner within sight of the pyramids.

  3. Fri 13 AugDepart

    Cairo to London

    Flight home to London.

14 to 30 August: patrons are at leisure before the Black Moon performance.

Chapter V

The Black Moon

  1. Tue 31 AugLondon

    The Black Moon

    The second new moon of August arrives at 6:41 pm BST. At dusk, Antony and Cleopatra is performed beside Cleopatra's Needle on the Victoria Embankment, with the obelisk lit against the darkest sky of the month. A reception for patrons follows.

Before you travel

Safe viewing in the August sun

Totality falls at midday in one of the hottest places on Earth in its hottest month. The expedition is planned around that.

Eye safety

Every patron receives certified eclipse glasses meeting ISO 12312-2. Looking at any part of the uncovered sun without them causes lasting eye damage. Glasses come off only during totality, on the guide's call, and go back on before the sun returns.

Heat

Midday temperatures in Luxor in August can reach 43°C (109°F). Viewing takes place from a shaded, air-conditioned base with water and rest areas, and touring is scheduled for early morning and evening.

Medical care

A medical team travels with the expedition in Luxor and on the river. Patrons are asked to share any conditions that the heat or long days could affect.

Insurance

Comprehensive travel and medical insurance, including evacuation cover, is required for all Eclipse and Expedition Circle patrons.

Passports and visas

A passport valid for at least six months is needed, and most visitors need a visa for Egypt. Details will be sent to registered patrons.

Licensed travel

Flights, hotels and the vessel will be arranged and sold by a licensed tour operator, with the financial protection required for package holidays sold in the UK.
